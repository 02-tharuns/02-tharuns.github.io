## Why industrial robotics and manufacturing roles fit his background
His Bachelor of Engineering in Industrial and Production Engineering (The
National Institute of Engineering, completed September 2022) supplies
manufacturing-floor grounding a pure computer-science background does not:
statistical quality control, metrology and measurement systems, control
engineering, mechatronics, and a CNC and robotics laboratory. Paired with a Master of
Science in Artificial Intelligence, he understands both the physical
production process and the ML layer built on top of it, which is the
intersection industrial robotics and manufacturing-automation roles need.

## Physical systems and condition monitoring
Three shipped projects work directly with physical hardware rather than only
with pre-collected datasets: the Smart Brake and Wheel-End Condition
Monitor's rotor thermal simulation and drag-torque inversion from cooling
curves; DARE-PM's on-device predictive maintenance running on real vibration
and acoustic streams; and the Edge Sensing Pipeline and Serverless Digital
Twin, deployed against physical Raspberry Pi hardware rather than a
simulator. All three are instances of the same manufacturing-floor problem:
deciding, from a physical sensor signal, whether equipment is degrading
before it fails.

## Career direction toward robotics and robot learning
He is moving from sensor and time-series machine learning toward robotics
and robot learning, and describes the bridge explicitly as data quality:
deciding automatically which sensor data, or which demonstration, is
trustworthy enough to train on — the same evaluation discipline
(session-held-out splits, reporting a metric downward rather than keeping an
indefensible number) he already applies to DARE-PM, the brake project, and
the vehicle-speed work.
